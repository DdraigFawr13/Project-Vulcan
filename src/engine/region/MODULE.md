# region

The region around the valley (P1.5's screen; since P1.6 a window of the seed's Story Generator world, DECISIONS V-031): its named neighbours and the three valleys on offer.

- **Public API:** `index.ts`: `neighboursOf(seed, content)` (`record.ts`, P5.9: each valley neighbour's reasons to leave from its last `strangers.years` years, by `strangers.reasons` kind, with the record's line), `makeRegion(seed, content, withRaster = false)` (pure; the player's seed without a valley number), `siteOf(seed)` (`<seed>/<n>` gives the region seed and valley `n`; a plain seed gives undefined), `valleyOf(seed, content)` (the region and the valley a world seed settles), `regionSystem`, and the types `Region`, `RegionCell`, `RegionRange`, `RegionSite`, `RegionValley`, `RegionNeighbour`. `Neighbour.kind` is the neighbour site's world kind (hamlet, village, town, city, fortress, hold, mine, temple, port).
- **The world:** `story.ts` is the adapter to the `story-generator` package (pinned to one commit in package.json): the package's content with Vulcan's races and cultures laid over its own (its ids kept, its old race ids read as Vulcan's through `region.json` `world.races`), checked by the package's own validator, then `generateWorld` at `world.size` cells and `world.years` of history. The last four worlds are kept per content object, so the picker, the valleys and the Region panel make each seed's world once (about 1.5 s). `story-generator.d.ts` types only the part read; tsconfig `paths` points the package's imports there, so its own source is checked by its own CI.
- **Generator:** `ground.ts` ranks windows of `world.frame` cells (six to eight live sites inside the site inset, two or more peoples, mostly land, then the most peoples and realms) and samples one onto the 80 by 56 grid: bilinear height (metres over `world.metres`), warmth and rain, and the biome under a noise-warped point so the world's cells don't show. `places.ts` reads the world's rivers (downstream, bent by the same warp), ranges and sites: name, people, the world's word for it (`kind`), the race glyph, the realm and its rule, faith, founding year and its most telling event of `history.importance` or more, in the package's words, a different kind of event per neighbour where it can and at most `history.length` characters where it can. `region.ts` tries up to `world.tries` windows and keeps the first with two or more peoples whose three valleys (low ground walled by heights, at least `valleys.clear` cells from a site) each take a different terrain.json climate; valleys are named in the nearest site's tongue (`valesBy`, else `vales`). No roots or trigonometry (V-006).
- **World seeds:** a world seed `<seed>/<n>` is valley `n` of region `<seed>`: every stream derives from that whole string, so the three valleys differ in everything, and the map generator takes the valley's climate (`valleyOf` in `map.ts`). A plain seed (tests, tools, worlds from before P1.5) has no region.
- **Events:** emits `region.named` (the written ranges and the rivers, and the site count) and `region.settled` (the valley, its range, climate and nearest neighbours), both citing `world.begun` (no "why?"), in `init`, only for a valley seed; reads none.
- **State owned:** none. The region is made again from the seed when asked, so saves do not carry it.
- **Invariants:** the same seed and content give the same world and region; site names are unique within a region, valley names unique among the three; every valley's climate is a terrain.json climate id.
- **Tests:** `region.test.ts`: the P1.5 and P1.6 done-when (the map seeds give distinct, named neighbourhoods of six to eight sites from two or more peoples, three named valleys each with the nearest three 2 to 12 days away), the same world made twice from scratch, a valley seed settles with its climate and its two lines, a plain seed has none. About 20 s: it makes eleven worlds.
- **Tools:** Node can't strip types inside `node_modules`, so `tools/engine.mjs` loads the engine for `map:stats` through Vite's module runner, and Vitest inlines the package.
